CASE 01 / DESIGN JUDGMENT

LINA — from literal information to cinematic emotion.

The strongest improvement was not a new tool. It was a better decision about what the frame needed to communicate.

ACTUAL ITERATION CLIPS

DIAGNOSIS

Clear information, limited emotional depth.

The original beat asked UI text to carry the rejection. The viewer understood the result, but did not experience Lina’s isolation.

DESIGN DECISION

Make the environment tell the story first.

I shifted the sequence toward spatial contrast, camera movement, passing professionals, scale, and reaction—so exclusion is felt before the phone result is read.

View detailed shot redesign

INITIAL DIRECTION

Lina exits the corporate tower.

Her phone vibrates.

214 MATCHES → 31 MATCHES → 0 MATCHES

Lina stops.

REFINED SHOT PLAN

  1. 01Front medium close-up: hold disappointment against the corporate tower.
  2. 02Move from frontal to profile; crane upward until Lina dissolves into the city crowd.
  3. 03Cut to Lina seated outside while confident professionals pass and discuss work.
  4. 04Low 45° phone angle: let sadness shift into surprise through performance, not exposition.

EVALUATION FRAMEWORK

How I decide whether a visual is ready.

My tools change. The review criteria remain consistent.

01

Composition

Clear subject, spatial relationship, intentional balance.

02

Hierarchy

A deliberate order of attention, not competing visual signals.

03

Continuity

Character, environment, props, screen geography, and action stay coherent.

04

Emotional Clarity

Performance and framing carry the feeling before exposition does.

05

Consistency

Styling, materials, lighting, typography, and generated details share one visual system.

06

Production Fitness

The output is usable, repeatable, editable, and right for the final format.
